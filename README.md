# Social Media Impact on Students' Daily Life 📱📊

## Project Overview

This project explores the impact of social media on students' daily lives, focusing on platform preferences, usage patterns, academic performance, sleep habits, and mental health.

The dataset was obtained from Kaggle and contains **4,500 student records and 16 original columns**. One additional column, *Mental Health Comparison*, was created during the analysis, bringing the total to **17 columns**.

The dataset was already clean when obtained, so no major data-cleaning procedures were required. The main focus was on data analysis, creating PivotTables, visualizing patterns, and interpreting findings using Microsoft Excel.

## Project Objectives

- Identify the most and least popular social media platforms among students.
- Examine social media usage across gender, age, and education levels.
- Explore the relationship between daily usage and academic performance.
- Investigate the relationship between late-night social media use, sleep, and mental health.
- Identify the potential benefits and drawbacks of social media in students' lives.
- Develop recommendations for responsible social media use.

## Key Findings

### 1. Most Popular Social Media Platforms

Instagram was the most popular platform, with **1,453 students**, while LinkedIn was the least popular, with **96 students**.

These findings indicate that students in this dataset preferred Instagram over LinkedIn.

### 2. Social Media Usage by Gender

The dataset recorded 2,234 female students and 2,051 male students in the gender comparison. Female students represented the larger group, and Instagram was the most popular platform among both genders.

### 3. Social Media Usage by Education Level

Undergraduates formed the largest education group, with **2,777 students**. This suggests that undergraduate students represented a substantial proportion of the dataset.

### 4. Social Media Usage by Age

Students aged 18 were reported to have higher social media usage than students in the other age groups. This may reflect differences in platform preferences and usage habits across ages.

### 5. Social Media and Academic Performance

The analysis suggests a relationship between longer daily social media usage and lower academic performance scores. Excessive usage may reduce the time available for studying, completing assignments, and maintaining effective study routines.

However, this relationship does not establish that social media use directly causes lower grades.

### 6. Late-Night Usage, Sleep, and Mental Health

Late-night social media use was examined in relation to sleep patterns, sleep quality, and mental health. The findings suggest that extended nighttime usage may be associated with poorer sleep habits and differences in academic performance and mental health scores.

Further statistical analysis would be required to establish the strength of these relationships.

### 7. Benefits and Drawbacks of Social Media

Social media offers several potential benefits to students, including:

- Access to educational materials and research resources.
- Opportunities to learn new skills and share knowledge.
- Networking and job-search opportunities.
- Communication and collaboration with other students.

However, excessive use may be associated with reduced study time, disrupted sleep, mental health concerns, and exposure to harmful or inappropriate online content.

The analysis reported 82% benefits, 15% drawbacks, and 4% other outcomes.

## Recommendations

1. **Encourage balanced social media use:** Students should set reasonable daily usage limits to maintain a healthy balance between online activities, studying, and other responsibilities.
2. **Reduce late-night usage:** Students should consider limiting social media use before bedtime to support healthy sleep routines.
3. **Promote digital well-being:** Parents and educators should guide younger students on responsible platform use, online safety, and healthy digital habits.
4. **Use social media for learning:** Students should take advantage of educational communities, research resources, professional networks, and legitimate job opportunities.
5. **Promote online safety:** Schools, parents, and platforms should help students recognize harmful content, misinformation, cyberbullying, and online risks.

## Tools Used

- **Microsoft Excel** — data analysis, PivotTables, calculated columns, and charts.
- **Kaggle** — dataset source.
- **GitHub** — project documentation and portfolio presentation.

## Conclusion

This project examines how social media usage patterns relate to students' academic performance, sleep, mental health, and daily activities. Instagram emerged as the most popular platform in the dataset, while undergraduates represented the largest education group.

The analysis highlights the potential of social media as a tool for learning, research, networking, and career development. At the same time, it raises concerns about excessive daily usage and late-night activity.

The overall conclusion is that **social media can be beneficial when used responsibly, but balanced usage and healthy digital habits are important for students' academic and personal well-being**.

Because this project is based on observational data, the findings indicate patterns and associations rather than proving cause-and-effect relationships.
